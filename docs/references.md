# References

Keys used across the docs. Each entry was checked against a publisher,
Crossref record, journal or institutional page (see
[reviews/review-sources.md](reviews/review-sources.md) for the check). Rich-list
and top-tail references are also in [notes/rich-lists.md](notes/rich-lists.md).

## Frameworks

- **[SSF2009]** Stiglitz, J., Sen, A., Fitoussi, J.-P. (2009). *Report by the
  Commission on the Measurement of Economic Performance and Social Progress.*
  Paris. Recommends a household perspective and looking jointly at income,
  consumption and wealth, including their distribution.
- **[OECD2013]** OECD (2013). *OECD Framework for Statistics on the
  Distribution of Household Income, Consumption and Wealth.* OECD Publishing.
  doi:10.1787/9789264194830-en.

## Distributional National Accounts (DINA)

- **[PSZ2018]** Piketty, T., Saez, E., Zucman, G. (2018). "Distributional
  National Accounts: Methods and Estimates for the United States."
  *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 133(2): 553–609. doi:10.1093/qje/qjx043.
- **[GGP2018]** Garbinti, B., Goupille-Lebret, J., Piketty, T. (2018). "Income
  Inequality in France, 1900–2014: Evidence from Distributional National
  Accounts (DINA)." *Journal of Public Economics* 162: 63–77.
  doi:10.1016/j.jpubeco.2018.01.012.
- **[GGP2021]** Garbinti, B., Goupille-Lebret, J., Piketty, T. (2021).
  "Accounting for Wealth-Inequality Dynamics: Methods, Estimates, and
  Simulations for France." *Journal of the European Economic Association*
  19(1): 620–663. doi:10.1093/jeea/jvaa025. A correction was published in the
  same issue (p. 707).
- **[GGP2016-H]** Garbinti, B., Goupille-Lebret, J., Piketty, T. (2016, rev.
  January 2017). Data appendix, WID Working Paper 2016/6, Appendix H
  (comparison with the Challenges rich list: families of 1, 10 or 15 members,
  80% married, about 20% of wealth held by non-residents). The project's
  rich-list scenarios are *inspired by* these parameters.
- **[GGP2016-T1]** Garbinti, B., Goupille-Lebret, J., Piketty, T. (2016, rev.
  January 2017). "Accounting for Wealth Inequality Dynamics: Methods,
  Estimates and Simulations for France (1800–2014)." WID Working Paper
  2016/5, Table 1 (2014: poorest 50% 6.3%, middle 40% 38.4%, top 0.001%
  0.9% of wealth).
- **[DINA-G]** Chancel, L., Flores, I., Moshrif, R., Nievas, G., Piketty, T.
  (coord.). *Distributional National Accounts (DINA) Guidelines: Methods and
  Concepts Used in the World Inequality Database*, version October 2025 (first
  version Alvaredo et al., WID WP 2016/2; revised 2020 and 2021 with Blanchet,
  Chancel, Flores, Morgan as coordinators). p. 70: "close to lump-sum
  imputation of healthcare expenditure and a proportional imputation of all
  other items".
- **[BFP2022]** Blanchet, T., Fournier, J., Piketty, T. (2022). "Generalized
  Pareto Curves: Theory and Applications." *Review of Income and Wealth*
  68(1): 263–288. doi:10.1111/roiw.12510. Code: <https://wid.world/gpinter>.
- **[TN2025-01]** Bajard, F., Bauluz, L., Brassac, P., Chancel, L.,
  Martínez-Toledano, C., Piketty, T., Sodano, A. (2025, updated 18 Dec 2025).
  "Global wealth inequality on WID.world: Estimates and imputations." World
  Inequality Lab Technical Note 2025/01, §3.3 pp. 4–5 (Forbes harmonised
  1988–2024; top g-percentile raised to Forbes totals).
  <https://wid.world/document/global-wealth-inequality-on-wid-world-estimates-and-imputations-world-inequality-lab-technical-note-2025-01/>
- **[TN2021-16]** Blanchet, T., Martínez-Toledano, C. (2021). "Distributional
  Wealth Accounts in Europe: Methodology." World Inequality Lab Technical Note
  2021/16 (WID France wealth after 2014: asset mix by percentile fixed at 2014).
- **[FPG2023]** Fisher-Post, M., Gethin, A. (2023). "Government Redistribution
  and Development: Global Estimates of Tax-and-Transfer Progressivity,
  1980–2019." WID Working Paper 2023/17 (source of WID's French post-tax
  series in this vintage).
- **[BGGGP2024]** Bozio, A., Garbinti, B., Goupille-Lebret, J., Guillot, M.,
  Piketty, T. (2024). "Predistribution versus Redistribution: Evidence from
  France and the United States." *American Economic Journal: Applied
  Economics* 16(2): 31–65. doi:10.1257/app.20220023. Baseline: health
  lump-sum, education and collective spending proportional.
- **[BCG2022]** Blanchet, T., Chancel, L., Gethin, A. (2022). "Why Is Europe
  More Equal than the United States?" *American Economic Journal: Applied
  Economics* 14(4): 480–518. doi:10.1257/app.20200703.

## Critiques and alternative estimates

- **[AS2024]** Auten, G., Splinter, D. (2024). "Income Inequality in the
  United States: Using Tax Data to Measure Long-Term Trends." *Journal of
  Political Economy* 132(7): 2179–2227. doi:10.1086/728741. The main critique
  of DINA allocation choices (US).
- **[SZ2020]** Saez, E., Zucman, G. (2020). "Trends in US Income and Wealth
  Inequality: Revising After the Revisionists." NBER Working Paper 27921. Reply
  to Auten & Splinter.
- **[Landais2007]** Landais, C. (2007). "Les hauts revenus en France
  (1998–2006) : une explosion des inégalités ?" Paris School of Economics
  working paper, June 2007.
  <https://insee.fr/fr/statistiques/fichier/2408296/landais_texte.pdf>
- **[CEFC]** France Stratégie, *Comité d'évaluation des réformes de la
  fiscalité du capital*, reports 2019–2023 (tax-data evidence on top
  dividends after 2018).
  <https://www.vie-publique.fr/sites/default/files/rapport/pdf/276598.pdf>
